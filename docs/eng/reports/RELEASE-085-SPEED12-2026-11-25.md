# Qeli 0.8.5 Beta: TCP P1/P4 throughput and UDP ceilings — 25 November 2026

[Русская версия](../../ru/reports/RELEASE-085-SPEED12-2026-11-25.md)

The lab completed **96 TCP measurements** and confirmed **48 UDP cells** over 12 profiles. Peak receiver goodput was **2,096.84 Mbit/s** on TCP P1 (`tcp-padding`, inner IPv4 download) and **1,990.08 Mbit/s** on TCP P4 (`tcp-reality`, inner IPv4 download). UDP traffic **inside a TCP tunnel** peaked at **1,861.47 Mbit/s** at **1,875 Mbit/s** offered load (`tcp-plain-raw`, inner IPv4 download). The best **outer UDP transport** reached **1,123.57 Mbit/s** at **1,125 Mbit/s** offered load (`udp-padding`, inner IPv6 upload).

## Build and lab

- Both VMs used the same portable Linux amd64 ELF from candidate `74312bf858430ca5318611f49e977c1cd409ea6e`. SHA-256 `e5821a1cb5ae904963ab4ce8dbe93ad20534ef5712fa78e587f43cdee53928c4` matched `SHA256SUMS`, both VMs and the source distribution after testing.
- The client and server had 2 vCPUs and 2 GiB RAM each on an Intel Core i5-8500, with Debian kernel `6.12.111+deb13-amd64` and iperf3 3.18. The outer path was IPv4; IPv4 and IPv6 were tested inside the tunnel. Upload means client → server. The server used `fq_codel` and the client used `fq` with `flow_limit=100`.
- Recordizer was `required` with `PACKET_MUX_V1` and `opportunistic` batching. TUN MTU was 1400 and TX queue length was 1000 at each end. Gateway, DNS and auto-reconnect were off. The Reality-TLS profile used genuine HTTP/2 and traffic shaping, with padding, stealth and heartbeat off.
- Temporary receive limits were 4 MiB, matching the Qeli socket request. The observed receive buffer was at least 8 MiB for Qeli and 4 MiB for the UDP iperf3 receiver. This is not a measurement of the package's 16 MiB auto profile. The original limits were restored.

## Method and limits

TCP ran once for each profile × inner IP family × direction × stream count: 10 seconds after a two-second warmup. P4 is the sum of four streams. UDP used one stream and 1,200-byte payloads. A six-second search refined each offered rate to a 125 Mbit/s step, capped by direct calibration and an 8 Gbit/s safety limit. Two separate 12-second confirmation windows had to meet both **at most 1% receiver loss** and **at least 95% of requested sender throughput**. Of 48 cells, 38 held the initial step, nine dropped 125 Mbit/s and one dropped 250 Mbit/s. A confirmed step is not a theoretical or long-term ceiling.

The first run stopped when its controller ran out of space writing JSON. Four direct P1 windows were recovered from raw iperf logs. The continuation verified the lab state and started at P4 without changes to binary or network parameters. The two runs recorded 437 windows including controls and rejected steps. Direct TCP baselines varied by at most 1.71%. Three of four highest direct UDP steps did not reproduce at the end, illustrating variability at the limit. All 96 UDP confirmation windows were independently reconciled with raw output. Both VMs passed 16 post-run state checks. This is a local throughput measurement, not a WAN test or a mixed-load latency test.

The top confirmed `tcp-plain-raw` peak was nearly unchanged (1,860.08 → 1,861.47 Mbit/s at the same offered load). Differences in windows and step selection preclude a broad improvement or regression claim. The Russian report lists the saved raw-data and summary references.
